# Deliverable 4 — Target Customer Definition

## The One-Line Answer

**Mid-market and enterprise companies (200–5,000 employees) with at least three
departments producing policy documents, where no single person can keep every
policy consistent in their head.**

---

## 1. Company Profile

| Attribute | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| **Size** | 200–5,000 employees |
| **Revenue** | $20M–$500M annual |
| **Industries** | Professional services, financial services, healthcare, technology, manufacturing |
| **Structure** | Multiple departments (Legal, HR, Engineering, Security, Operations) each producing their own policy documents |
| **Document volume** | 20–500 active policy documents across the organization |
| **Current state** | Documents live in SharePoint, Google Drive, Confluence, or shared folders with no cross-referencing |

### Why this size?

- **Below 200 employees:** One HR manager or office manager can keep policies
  consistent manually. The pain is not severe enough to buy software.
- **Above 5,000 employees:** Enterprises at this scale already have GRC
  (Governance, Risk & Compliance) platforms. NEXORA competes on simplicity and
  speed-to-value, not on feature parity with ServiceNow or Workiva.
- **200–5,000 is the gap:** Big enough that policies contradict each other, small
  enough that nobody has a dedicated compliance team catching it.

---

## 2. The Buyer vs. The User

The person who signs the contract and the person who uses the product daily are
different people. Both must see value.

### The Buyer (Signs the contract)

| Role | Title examples | What they care about |
|------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Head of Operations / COO** | VP Operations, Chief of Staff | Reducing operational risk from policy contradictions |
| **General Counsel** | Head of Legal, VP Legal | Contractual consistency, audit readiness |
| **CHRO / Head of HR** | VP People, Head of People Ops | Employee handbook accuracy, onboarding consistency |

**What the buyer says yes to:** "We had an incident where an employee was told
two different things by two different policies. This tool makes sure that never
happens again."

### The Users (Use it daily)

NEXORA serves three user roles. Every role reads every document — roles change
what surfaces first, never what is visible.

| Role | Who they are | How they use NEXORA |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Employee** | Support staff, engineers, associates, new hires | Ask questions about company policies, get grounded answers with citations |
| **Department Owner** | HR lead, General Counsel, Engineering lead, Security lead | Review conflict detections in their department, approve resolutions |
| **Director** | CEO, VP, Director | See cross-department conflicts, approve org-wide policy changes |

## 3. What They Use Today (and What Breaks)

| Current tool | What it does | What breaks |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| **SharePoint / Google Drive** | Stores documents | No cross-referencing; two departments can contradict each other and nobody notices |
| **Confluence / Notion** | Wiki-style documentation | Same problem: contradictions between pages are invisible |
| **ChatGPT / Copilot with file upload** | Answers questions from uploaded files | Confidently picks one answer when sources disagree — spreads the wrong information with the authority of AI |
| **Manual policy reviews** | Annual or quarterly reviews by legal/compliance | Too slow, too infrequent; contradictions survive between reviews |
| **GRC platforms (ServiceNow, Workiva)** | Full compliance management | Expensive ($100K+/year), long implementation (6–12 months), overkill for document consistency |

### The specific pain

> "The Employee Handbook says 10 casual leave days. The HR Leave Policy says 12.
> The Manager Guide says 15. A new hire asks their manager, who checks one
> document and gives a confident wrong answer. The employee relies on it. When
> the discrepancy surfaces, trust is damaged — in the company, in the manager,
> and in the policy system."

This is not a hypothetical. Every company above ~200 people has experienced
some version of this. The question is how often, and how much it costs when it
goes wrong.

## 5. Worked Example: Acme Corp

**Acme Corp** — 800 employees, $80M revenue, technology company.

- **Departments producing policies:** HR (handbook, leave, onboarding), Legal
  (contracts, NDAs, compliance), Engineering (coding standards, release process),
  Security (incident response, data retention), Finance (expense policy).
- **Document count:** ~60 active policy documents.
- **Current state:** Documents in Google Drive and Confluence. Last
  comprehensive review was 14 months ago.
- **Known incidents:** Two contradictions discovered by employees in the past
  year (leave entitlement and equipment return deadline). Both required HR
  escalation and took 3 weeks to resolve.

**NEXORA value:** Continuous monitoring catches contradictions the day they are
introduced, not months later when someone trips over them. At $15/seat/month for
department owners and directors (15 seats), the annual cost is $2,700 — less
than the cost of a single HR escalation.
